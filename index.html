Step 1Start Here

Your group was assigned one place in Italy. Find out what is changing there, how it impacts daily life, and what people are doing in response. Begin with the library databases. The articles you find there are expert-edited and trustworthy, making them ideal for school research. Then visit the websites listed for your place.

Say It in Italian

Your poster must identify the climate problem in proper Italian. Don't guess and don't paste it into a translator! Search IATE (opens in a new tab), the European Union's terminology database, where EU translators record official terms in every EU language and rate how reliable each one is. Then make sure the term appears in an Italian source, like a city or government website.

Step 2The Basics

Start with an encyclopedia. Read for the basics: where your place is, what it's known for, and what typical weather is like. Jot down words worth searching later.

  • school.eb.comBritannica School (opens in a new tab)

    Articles at three reading levels. Switch levels at the top of any article. Each of the seven places has its own entry.

  • infobaselearning.comWorld Geography and Culture (opens in a new tab)

    Infobase's country profiles. Italy's covers cuisine, holidays and celebrations, and weather and climate. It's a good start for making your cultural connection!

  • culturegrams.comCultureGrams (opens in a new tab)

    Daily life, customs, and food in more than 200 countries and territories.

Cite as you go

Create a NoodleTools project before you start reading. In Britannica School, click Cite, then Export to NoodleTools. Check every field against the article before you move on.

Step 3Adding Context

Now find out what's happening. Gale In Context: Global Issues gathers news, viewpoints, and reference articles on world problems and sorts them into topic pages. Open the topic page listed for your place in step 4, then use Search within results to add your place's name.

  • gale.comGale In Context: Global Issues (opens in a new tab)

    Topic pages on climate, water, weather, and more, plus a page for every country. Filter by Lexile to find articles at a comfortable reading level.

  • gale.comGale Power Search (opens in a new tab)

    Searches several Gale databases at once. Useful when news about your place is held in more than one database.

Is It Climate Change?

Not every flood or fire is caused by climate change. Scientists can test whether warming made a single event more likely. Sometimes the answer is yes; sometimes it isn't clear. A good researcher reports what the evidence shows, even when it complicates the story. Emilia-Romagna's researchers will meet one of these surprises.

That said, climate change is settled science and its impacts myriad. You can simultaneously accept scientific consensus and exercise skepticism toward oversimplifications of complex, entangled systems!

Search smarter

Pair your place with your problem: Venice flooding, Sicily wildfires, Puglia drought. No results? Try a bigger place (Italy) or another word for the problem (heat wave, heat).

Cite as you go

Every Gale article has a Cite button. Choose MLA 9th Edition, then export to NoodleTools or copy the citation. Compare what arrives with the article itself.

  • Euronews, “Were Italy's devastating floods really caused by climate change?” (opens in a new tab) A short news report on a scientific inquiry.

Weigh the Evidence

Scientists studied the May 2023 rains and found no clear sign of climate change in them. They did link the drought before the floods to climate change, as that dry ground couldn’t soak up the first rain.

What actually caused the disaster? Read both reports, then decide how to tell this complicated story on your poster.
