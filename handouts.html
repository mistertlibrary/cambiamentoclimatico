Pair your place with your problem: Venice flooding, Sicily wildfires, Puglia drought. No results? Try a bigger place (Italy) or a different term for the problem (heat wave, heat).

Say It in Italian

Your poster must refer to the climate problem in Italian. Don't guess at the correct term and don't paste “climate change” into a translator! Search IATE, the European Union's terminology database, and determine what word or phrase is most appropriate. Pay close attention to the terminology Italian-language websites employ!

Is It Climate Change?

Weather disasters are often related to climate change, but we can't attribute every event directly to these broader shifts. Scientists can assess whether warming contributed to an event, but limitations on available data and our understanding of complex systems complicate these determinations. As a researcher, your role is to share both what the evidence shows and how confident experts are in their hypotheses, even if doing so tangles a straightforward narrative.

That said, climate change is settled science; its effects are myriad, its impacts severe. You can simultaneously acknowledge scientific consensus and exercise skepticism toward oversimplifications of complex, entangled systems!

  • Euronews, “Were Italy's devastating floods really caused by climate change?” euronews.com (search the title)
    A short news report about a scientific inquiry.
  • World Weather Attribution, “Limited net role for climate change in heavy spring rainfall in Emilia-Romagna.” worldweatherattribution.org
    The scientists' firsthand report; you can get by with reading the summary at the top.

Weigh the Evidence

Scientists studied the May 2023 rains and found no clear correlation between the volume of rainfall and climate change. They did link the drought before the floods to climate change, as that dry ground couldn’t soak up the earliest precipitation.

What factors contributed most directly to this disaster? Read both reports, then decide how to tell this complicated story on your poster.
